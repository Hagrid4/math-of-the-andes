About Our Project

Our project explores different ways mathematics can be applied in the real world, using the Andes Mountains as our focus. The project examines biodiversity and the ways in which mathematical models can be used to understand ecosystems and animal populations.

This website focuses specifically on two components: an interactive replica of a calculator that calculates Simpson’s Diversity Index (SDI), and a short, self-made documentary. The SDI calculator can be used to calculate the biodiversity of a region containing ‘n’ number of animals.

The documentary focuses mainly on three endangered animals found in the Andes Mountains: Chinchillas, Andean Mountain Cats, and Andean Condors. It covers the habitats of these endangered species, along with a short representation of the food chains within the region.

The remaining components of our project will be presented at our stall. These include information about the uses and significance of biodiversity, the Lotka–Volterra Model, a predator-and-prey population model, a topographic representation of the Andes Mountains in the form of both a poster and a diorama, and a large food web representing the food chains featured in the documentary.

Together, these components demonstrate how mathematics can be applied to understand and represent biodiversity, ecosystems, and the relationships between species in the Andes Mountains.

Simpson's Diversity Index

Simpson's Diversity Index is a measure of diversity which takes into account the number of species present, as well as the relative abundance of each species. As species richness and evenness increase, so diversity increases. Check out our interactive replica of the Simpson's Diversity Index Calculator here.

EXPLORE THE ANDES

Take a journey through the diverse landscapes of the Andes Mountains and discover the habitats of three remarkable endangered species: the chinchilla, Andean mountain cat, and Andean condor. Our short documentary explores how these animals live within their environments and how they are connected through the food chains of the region. Through visuals and storytelling, it brings the biodiversity of the Andes to life and gives you a closer look at the ecosystems our project explores.
